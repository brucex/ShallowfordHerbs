Yes, acupuncture can help some people with back pain. Pain, including back pain, is the most common reason Americans try it. Doctors' guidelines list it as one of the first non-drug options for long-lasting low back pain. It doesn't work for everyone, and it isn't a cure. ## What the research says about acupuncture for back pain

We'd rather give you the honest picture than a sales pitch.

### Doctors' guidelines include it

The American College of Physicians (ACP) published a [guideline on treating low back pain](https://www.acpjournals.org/doi/10.7326/M16-2367). For **chronic** low back pain (pain lasting more than 12 weeks), it recommends that patients and doctors start with non-drug treatments. Acupuncture is on that list, along with exercise, yoga, tai chi and others. For **acute** back pain (newer pain), acupuncture is listed as an option alongside heat, massage and spinal manipulation. The [ACP's announcement](https://www.acponline.org/acp-newsroom/american-college-of-physicians-issues-guideline-for-treating-nonradicular-low-back-pain) also notes that new back pain usually improves over time, whatever treatment you choose.

### The evidence is modest, and stronger for long-term pain

NCCIH's summary of [low back pain and complementary approaches](https://www.nccih.nih.gov/health/low-back-pain-and-complementary-health-approaches-what-you-need-to-know) describes the evidence as moderate quality for chronic low back pain and low quality for acute pain. These ratings describe confidence in the evidence, not the size of the benefit. Benefits vary, and the evidence is less certain for newer pain.

NCCIH also notes that in studies, acupuncture beats no treatment by more than it beats "sham" (fake) acupuncture. That suggests part of the benefit comes from things like the relaxing setting and the care you receive, not just the needles. We think it's fair for you to know that.

### Sciatica is less clear

If your pain shoots down your leg, that may be sciatica. NCCIH says acupuncture [may help sciatica pain](https://www.nccih.nih.gov/health/acupuncture-effectiveness-and-safety), but the research isn't strong enough to draw firm conclusions. Leg pain with numbness or weakness also needs a doctor's check first (see below).

## How Traditional Chinese Medicine looks at back pain

In Traditional Chinese Medicine (TCM), pain is often described as a blockage in the flow of Chi (also written Qi) through the body's pathways, called meridians. Dr. Xu likes to compare it to electricity: it needs to flow without short circuits.

Two people with "back pain" can have very different patterns. Pain that's worse in cold, damp weather is viewed differently from pain that comes with fatigue and a weak lower back, or sharp pain after a strain. That's why Dr. Xu asks a lot of questions before he treats you, and why your plan may not look like someone else's.

## How many sessions will you need?

It varies. Mayo Clinic notes that a [common treatment plan](https://www.mayoclinic.org/tests-procedures/acupuncture/about/pac-20392763) for a single problem is one or two sessions a week, and 6 to 8 treatments in total is common. Mayo also says that if your symptoms don't start to improve within a few weeks, acupuncture may not be right for you. If that happens, we'll talk it through with you honestly.

## When to see a doctor instead

Most back pain is not dangerous, but some symptoms need medical care first. Acupuncture should never delay that.

**Call 911 or go to the emergency room** if your back pain, as [Mayo Clinic lists](https://www.mayoclinic.org/symptoms/back-pain/basics/when-to-see-doctor/sym-20050878):

- Follows trauma, such as a car crash, bad fall or sports injury
- Comes with new bowel or bladder control problems
- Comes with a fever

**See your doctor** if your back pain hasn't improved after a week of home care, or if it:

- Is constant or intense, especially at night or when lying down
- Spreads down one or both legs, especially below the knee
- Causes weakness, numbness or tingling in one or both legs
- Comes with weight loss you can't explain
- Comes with swelling or a change in skin color on your back

Once your doctor has ruled out a serious cause, acupuncture can work alongside the care they recommend.

## Simple habits that help your back

- **Keep moving gently.** Mayo Clinic notes that [bed rest isn't recommended](https://www.mayoclinic.org/symptoms/back-pain/basics/when-to-see-doctor/sym-20050878) for back pain.
- **Try heat.** The ACP guideline lists superficial heat as an option for newer back pain.
- **Stay warm.** In TCM, cold and damp are said to settle into stiff joints and muscles. A warm layer on a chilly morning won't hurt.
- **Lift with care.** Bend your knees and keep heavy things close to your body.

## FAQ

### Is acupuncture good for lower back pain?

It can help some people. The ACP guideline includes acupuncture among the first non-drug treatments for chronic low back pain, and NCCIH rates the evidence for chronic low back pain as moderate quality. Results vary from person to person.

### Does acupuncture for back pain hurt?

Most people feel very little. You might notice a brief pinch or a dull, heavy feeling at a point. Tell Dr. Xu if anything feels uncomfortable.

### Can acupuncture help sciatica?

It may help, but NCCIH says the research isn't strong enough for firm conclusions. See your doctor first if leg pain comes with numbness, tingling or weakness.

### Can I keep taking my pain medicine?

Yes. Keep taking what your doctor prescribed, and don't stop a medication without talking to them first. Tell Dr. Xu what you take, and let him know if you're pregnant, nursing, have a pacemaker or take blood thinners.
