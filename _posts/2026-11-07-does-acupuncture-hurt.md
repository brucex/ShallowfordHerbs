## Why acupuncture feels nothing like a shot

The needles are the big difference.

- **They're very thin.** Acupuncture needles are much thinner than the needles used for shots or blood draws, according to [Cleveland Clinic](https://my.clevelandclinic.org/health/treatments/4767-acupuncture).
- **They're solid, not hollow.** A shot needle is a hollow tube built to push medicine in or draw blood out. An acupuncture needle is a fine, solid wire with nothing going in or out.
- **They're sterile and single-use.** The U.S. Food and Drug Administration regulates acupuncture needles as medical devices and requires them to be sterile and labeled for single use, as [NCCIH explains](https://www.nccih.nih.gov/health/acupuncture-effectiveness-and-safety).

Cleveland Clinic says the small prick of an acupuncture needle is less painful than getting a vaccine or having blood drawn. [Mayo Clinic](https://www.mayoclinic.org/tests-procedures/acupuncture/about/pac-20392763) says insertion usually causes little discomfort and people often don't feel the needles go in at all.

## What acupuncture actually feels like

Here's what you might notice during a session.

### When the needle goes in

Often nothing. Sometimes a light tap or a brief pinch. In an article on [what acupuncture really feels like](https://health.clevelandclinic.org/does-acupuncture-hurt), Cleveland Clinic explains that because most needles feel like nothing, the one you do feel can catch you by surprise. That pinch usually fades in a few seconds.

### Once the needle is in place

You may feel a dull ache, heaviness, warmth or a mild tingling around the point. Mayo Clinic describes a mild aching feeling when a needle reaches the right depth. In Chinese medicine this sensation has a name, *de qi* (pronounced "duh chee"), as the [British Acupuncture Council](https://acupuncture.org.uk/faqs/) explains. These sensations do not by themselves show whether treatment will help your symptoms. It should feel deep and dull, not sharp.

### While you rest

Many people feel calm, even sleepy. The [UK's University College London Hospitals](https://www.uclh.nhs.uk/patients-and-visitors/patient-information-pages/having-acupuncture) describes the feeling around the needle as warm and heavy, and "usually quite pleasant."

### When the needles come out

Mayo Clinic notes that there's usually no discomfort when needles are removed.

## Why some spots feel more than others

Not every point feels the same. Cleveland Clinic notes that some areas tend to be more sensitive:

- **Fingers, toes and ears**, which have more nerve endings
- **Muscles**, where you may feel heaviness or a twitch
- **Sore or inflamed areas**, such as a recent injury
- **Small blood vessels**, which can leave a drop of blood or two

## What to expect afterward

Most people feel relaxed, and some feel energized. Common, minor side effects include:

- Soreness where a needle was placed
- Minor bleeding or a small bruise
- Feeling tired or a bit drowsy for a while

Mayo Clinic lists soreness and minor bleeding or bruising as the common side effects. University College London Hospitals lists short-lived drowsiness as common too. Cleveland Clinic suggests resting for 5 to 10 minutes before you drive, especially after your first visit, since acupuncture can be calming. Serious complications are rare when acupuncture is done by a trained practitioner with sterile needles, according to NCCIH.

## How to make your first session more comfortable

- **Eat a light meal beforehand.** University College London Hospitals notes that fainting is rare but can happen, particularly at a first treatment. The British Acupuncture Council asks patients to say if they're hungry or thirsty.
- **Wear loose clothes** so your arms and legs are easy to reach.
- **Say you're nervous.** Dr. Xu can explain each step before he does it.
- **Breathe slowly** as each needle goes in.
- **Speak up.** If a needle pinches or aches more than you like, tell him. It can be adjusted or removed.

## Who should check first

Before acupuncture, tell Dr. Xu if you:

- Are pregnant or nursing
- Have a pacemaker
- Take blood thinners or have a bleeding disorder

Mayo Clinic points out that some acupuncture points are thought to stimulate labor, and that electrical stimulation on needles could interfere with a pacemaker. And as NCCIH reminds people, don't use acupuncture to put off seeing a doctor about a health problem.

## FAQ

### Does acupuncture hurt the first time?

Most first-timers feel less than they expect. The first session may feel more noticeable simply because everything is new. Tell Dr. Xu if you're nervous, and he'll walk you through it.

### How thin are acupuncture needles?

Much thinner than the needles used for shots or blood draws. They're solid, not hollow, which is a big reason they feel so different.

### Is it normal to feel a dull ache?

Yes. A dull ache, heaviness or tingling at the point is common, and Mayo Clinic describes a mild aching feeling when a needle reaches the right depth. A sharp or burning pain that doesn't fade isn't something to put up with. Tell Dr. Xu.

### Will I bleed or bruise?

Sometimes you'll see a tiny drop of blood or a small bruise. Both are common and minor.

### Can I drive home afterward?

Most people can. Cleveland Clinic suggests resting for 5 to 10 minutes first, especially after your first visit.

### What if I'm afraid of needles?

You're not alone, and it's fine to say so. Dr. Xu can explain each step and go at your pace.
