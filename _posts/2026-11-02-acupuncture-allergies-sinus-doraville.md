## Why sinus trouble can linger into winter in Atlanta

Outdoor pollen drops off once the weather cools, but Atlanta's fall and winter bring their own triggers. We close the windows and turn on the heat. Dust, pet dander and indoor mold have more time to build up, and heated air dries out the nose and sinuses. Then come colds that seem to "settle in" and never quite clear.

So some people go from fall allergies straight into a winter of congestion, post-nasal drip and sinus pressure without a break in between.

## How Traditional Chinese Medicine looks at allergies and sinus problems

Traditional Chinese Medicine (TCM) looks past the symptom to ask why the body is reacting this way in the first place.

In TCM, the **Lung** system governs breathing, the nose and the skin. It's also tied to what's called **Wei Qi**, often translated as "defensive energy," which you can think of as the body's protective layer against wind, cold and other outside influences. When Wei Qi is weak, the idea is that the body overreacts to things it should be able to handle, and you get the sneezing, itching and runny nose that come with allergies.

Sinus trouble is often described in terms of **dampness** or **heat** collecting in the head. Thick, colored mucus, pressure behind the eyes and a dull headache point one way. Clear, watery drainage and sneezing in cold air point another. That's why two people with "sinus problems" may leave our clinic with very different treatment plans.

The aim is to treat the immediate discomfort and also strengthen the system underneath, so your body is less reactive over time.

## What a visit for allergies or sinus looks like

If you've read our [guide to your first acupuncture visit](/blog/first-acupuncture-visit-doraville-ga), much of this will sound familiar.

**A conversation and traditional diagnosis.** Dr. Xu will ask about your symptoms, when they flare, what the mucus is like, how you sleep, and whether you get frequent colds. He'll check your pulse at both wrists and look at your tongue. These give him clues about where the imbalance lies.

**Acupuncture, if it fits.** Very fine needles are placed at points chosen for your pattern. Some are near the nose and face, others on the hands, arms or legs. Most patients feel very little and many find it relaxing.

**An individualized herbal prescription.** This is the heart of how our clinic has treated allergies for generations. Instead of a one-size-fits-all pill, Dr. Xu writes a formula for you, and the raw herbs are measured into bags you brew into tea at home. He'll explain exactly how to prepare it. We'll be honest: it won't taste like dessert, but most people get used to it quickly.

**Follow-up.** Allergies and chronic sinus problems usually build up over years, so they're best approached as a course of care rather than a single visit. Dr. Xu checks in with patients between visits and adjusts the herbs as things change.

## Simple habits that support your sinuses

Alongside treatment, a few everyday steps can help:

- **Keep indoor air in check.** Change HVAC filters on schedule, wash bedding in hot water, and keep humidity moderate, not bone-dry and not damp.
- **Stay warm around the neck and head.** In TCM, cold and wind are said to enter easily there. A scarf on a chilly morning at the MARTA station is good advice in any tradition.
- **Go easy on cold, greasy and very sweet foods** if you notice they leave you more congested. TCM associates them with dampness.
- **Rest when you're run down.** A tired body tends to be a more reactive body.

## When to see your doctor first

Acupuncture and herbs can work alongside conventional care, but some symptoms need a medical evaluation. See your physician or urgent care for a high fever, severe facial pain or swelling, vision changes, a stiff neck, symptoms lasting more than a few weeks without improvement, or any trouble breathing. If you use an inhaler or allergy medication, keep using it as prescribed and bring a list of your medications to your visit.

## FAQ

### Can acupuncture help with allergies?

Many of our patients seek acupuncture and herbs for allergies, and some research suggests acupuncture may help with allergy symptoms for some people. Results vary from person to person. Dr. Xu will give you an honest view of what to expect for your situation after your consultation.

### Do I need both acupuncture and Chinese herbs?

Not always. For allergies and sinus trouble, herbs are often a big part of the plan, but Dr. Xu recommends whatever combination fits your pattern.

### How long before I notice a difference?

It depends on how long you've had the problem and how your body responds. Some people notice changes early, while long-standing sinus trouble usually takes longer. Dr. Xu will talk this through with you.

### Is it too late in the year to start?

No. Fall and winter are a good time to work on chronic sinus trouble, and starting before spring pollen arrives gives your body time to get stronger before the next season.

### Can I keep taking my allergy medicine?

Yes. Keep taking anything your doctor prescribed, and don't stop a medication without talking to them first. Tell Dr. Xu what you take, and let him know if you're pregnant, nursing or on blood thinners.
