## How Traditional Chinese Medicine looks at your health

In TCM, good health depends on the smooth flow of Chi (also written Qi), the body's energy, along pathways called meridians. When that flow is blocked or out of balance, the idea is that discomfort and illness can follow. Dr. Xu likes to compare it to electricity: it needs to flow without short circuits.

Acupuncture and Chinese herbs are two tools for helping restore that balance so your body can do its own healing. TCM rarely offers instant relief. The goal is to work on the root of the problem over a course of care, not just quiet the symptoms for a day.

## Step 1: The consultation

Your first visit starts with a conversation. Dr. Xu will ask about your main concern, how long it has been going on, what makes it better or worse, and how you're sleeping, eating and feeling overall.

Then comes **traditional diagnosis**. Dr. Xu will:

- **Feel your pulse** at both wrists. In TCM, the pulse gives a lot of information beyond your heart rate.
- **Look at your tongue.** Its color, coating and shape are important clues.
- **Check your eyes and nails.**

It's quick, painless and nothing to prepare for. It does help to bring a list of any medications or supplements you take.

## Step 2: Acupuncture, if it's right for you

If acupuncture is part of your plan, Dr. Xu places very fine needles at specific points along your meridians. These aren't like the needles used for shots or blood draws. They are much thinner, and most people feel only a light tap or a dull, heavy sensation, if they feel anything at all. Many patients find the treatment relaxing.

Wear loose, comfortable clothing so your arms and legs are easy to reach. If you're nervous, just say so. Dr. Xu will explain what he's doing as he goes.

## Step 3: Your herbal prescription

This is where our clinic is a little different from many others. Dr. Xu has been practicing Chinese herbal medicine the traditional way for Atlanta patients since 1987. Instead of pills or powders, Dr. Xu writes an **individualized herbal prescription** based on your diagnosis. A precise mix of herbs is measured into bags for you to take home.

At home, you boil each bag down into several cups of herbal "tea" and drink it the prescribed number of times per day. Dr. Xu will explain exactly how to brew it.

We'll be honest about the taste. As Dr. Xu tells patients: "Most brews do not taste bad, but they rarely taste good either. Hold your breath and drink it down fast."

## Step 4: Follow-up

TCM works over time, so you'll usually come back for follow-up visits. Dr. Xu also follows up with phone calls to check on how you're doing between visits. How many visits you need depends on your condition and how your body responds, and he'll talk that through with you honestly.

## Frequently asked questions

### Does acupuncture hurt?

Most people are surprised by how little they feel. Acupuncture needles are very thin. You might notice a brief pinch, tingling or a heavy, achy feeling at the point, which usually fades quickly. Tell Dr. Xu if anything feels uncomfortable.

### Do I have to take herbs and get acupuncture?

Not necessarily. Some patients receive both, others mostly herbs or mostly acupuncture. Dr. Xu recommends what fits your condition after the consultation.

### Can I keep taking my regular medications?

Keep taking anything your doctor has prescribed, and bring a list of your medications and supplements to your visit so Dr. Xu can take them into account. Don't stop or change a prescription without talking to the doctor who prescribed it.

### Is there anything I should tell Dr. Xu first?

Yes. Before starting herbs or acupuncture, tell Dr. Xu if you're pregnant, nursing, or taking blood thinners.

### How soon will I feel a difference?

It varies. Some people notice changes early, while long-standing problems usually take longer. TCM is aimed at the root of the problem, so it's best to think in terms of a course of care rather than a single visit.
